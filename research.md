# Research

My research connects fungal biology with practical approaches to forest disease detection and management.

## Molecular diagnostics for forest pathogens

I develop and evaluate molecular tools for detecting tree pathogens, including loop-mediated isothermal amplification (LAMP) assays for pitch canker and oak wilt. My doctoral research also explores Nanopore multi-gene target-capture sequencing as an approach to identifying the pathogens associated with needle diseases of southern pines.

## Population genomics and reproductive biology

I investigate genomic structure and historical patterns of migration in populations of *Fusarium circinatum*, the pathogen responsible for pine pitch canker. Related work examines mating types, outcrossing, and female infertility to better understand the organism’s reproductive biology.

## Fungal systematics and insect associations

I study the identities and evolutionary relationships of pine- and pine beetle-associated *Leptographium* sensu lato in Georgia. This work combines phylogenetic analysis with the study of fungal characteristics to reassess diversity in these forest-associated fungi.

## Disease resistance and rapid phenotyping

I contribute to research on loblolly pine resistance to brown spot needle blight, including rapid phenotyping using near-infrared vibrational spectroscopy. This work complements efforts to improve disease screening and characterize the genetic basis of resistance.

## Collaborative molecular ecology

I collaborate on projects using DNA-based methods to examine biodiversity and trophic interactions, including the diets of bats, birds, and Joro spiders. These collaborations extend molecular tools from pathogen detection to broader ecological questions.
